Overall

The direct manipulation is the best motion in the app. The card follows the finger, coasts, pivots and gets caught. The weak spots are the hand-offs between screens. The title leaves with more movement than it arrived with, the shimmer cuts off mid-sweep, the counter's big number vanishes, and the home screen fades everything in the same way. None of it honours reduced motion.

Where the timings land

Each dot is a transition between states, plotted by roughly how long it takes to settle. Spring settle times are estimated from their stiffness and damping. Swiping and holding are left off the chart because they track the finger with no duration of their own.

Instant / Responsive · 0–300ms — taps, ticks, small surfaces.
Deliberate · 300–500ms — earned by big moves, like a card crossing the screen.
Sluggish · 500ms+ — fine for a fading decoration, never for anything you wait on.
What's off

The slowest moves are exits (1 and 2), at the moment you've just tapped Add and are waiting for the payoff. Exits should be the quickest part of a change. The shimmer (9) has no ending at all, and the home reveal (11) takes 400ms on a screen people open every day.

Issues to address
CriticalJakubEmil

None of the Framer Motion animation respects reduced motion

Whatglobals.css has a prefers-reduced-motion rule, but it only reaches CSS animations. Everything in the onboarding runs through Framer Motion, which ignores that rule unless it's told otherwise. That covers the flying cards, the spin carried from the hand, the 3D pitch, the swinging held card and the stack. There's no MotionConfig and no useReducedMotion anywhere.

Why it mattersA spinning, tilting card thrown across the screen is a classic vestibular trigger. Someone who has asked their phone for less motion still gets all of it.

Recommended motionWrap the onboarding in <MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">. With reduced motion on, a flicked card fades into its slot (opacity plus a 0.96 → 1 scale over 200ms) and doesn't fly. Set spin, tilt, pitch, swing, hang and the detent tick to zero. The swipe itself can stay, because it follows the finger.

CriticalJakub

Cards fold into the glow bars by animating width and height

WhatWhen the stack lands on the home screen, each card shrinks from 152×240 to the 4×24 glow bar by animating width, height and border-radius. Those are layout properties, so every frame of the fold recalculates layout and repaints while three cards and the home screen move at once.

Why it mattersThis is the climax of the video and the busiest moment on screen, which makes it the most likely place to drop frames in a screen recording.

Recommended motionKeep the element at card size and fold it with scaleX/scaleY toward the bar's size (4/152 and 24/240). Fade the face out early, as it does now, so the squeezed art never shows. Draw the rounded end with a clip-path: inset(… round …) instead of an animated radius.

Leaving the pick screen moves more than arriving did

WhatThe title and slots arrive with opacity and blur only, but leave with a 12px rise as well, over 400ms. The hand then drops 420px on a soft spring (stiffness 120, damping 20) that takes about 450ms to settle. Both play right after you tap Add, while you wait for the payoff.

Why it mattersThe user is moving on, so exits should get out of the way. Here the exits are the slowest moves in the flow and pull the eye away from the stack forming in the middle.

Recommended motionTitle and slots: opacity and a 2px blur, no movement, 200ms. Hand: drop 160px and fade, on a spring with no bounce and a ~300ms visual duration. The demo loops an enter, a hold and the quieter exit.

ImportantJakub

The loading shimmer cuts off mid-sweep

WhatThe light sweep across the stacked cards is a conditional render ({shimmer && …}) with no exit. When loading ends it disappears the same frame, wherever the sweep happens to be, just as the cards start moving to the glow bars.

Why it mattersA highlight that vanishes halfway across a card reads as a glitch, and it lands on the most-watched beat of the video.

Recommended motionWrap it in AnimatePresence and give it an exit that fades the sweep out over 150ms. Better still, stop the loop after the current pass and let that pass finish before the cards move.

ImportantJheyJakub

The payoff number vanishes instead of becoming the headline

WhatDuring loading, a large counter climbs to the yearly value found (about $3,110 for the three demo cards). It then blurs away, and the home screen opens on a different number, $0 / $1,315, which is value used against annual fees. The biggest number in the demo is never seen again.

Why it mattersThat counter is the shareable moment and the reason to watch to the end. Letting it disappear breaks continuity right where the story should land.

Recommended motionFly the counter up into the home header as the cards fold into their bars, using a shared layoutId or a measured transform. Scale it from 1.15 to 1 and let it settle as a line under the total, for example "$3,110 in benefits a year". The demo shows the move up into place.

Issues to address
ImportantEmilJakub

The home screen fades everything in the same way, every time it opens

WhatThe total, every card name and every benefit list share one CSS keyframe: 400ms, 8px rise, 4px blur, the built-in ease-out, staggered from 100ms by 75ms. It plays at the end of the onboarding and also on every normal app open, since animate-stagger applies whenever there is no intro.

Why it mattersOn a screen opened daily, a 400ms blur on every block delays reading each time. Applying the same treatment to every element also flattens the hierarchy, which the skill flags as blur-everywhere and uniform fade-in.

Recommended motionGive it a hierarchy. Only the total gets the blur materialize (220ms). Card names fade with a 4px rise, and rows fade with opacity only, each at 180ms on cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1) with a 30ms stagger. On ordinary app opens, skip the reveal entirely.

The gesture physics were just rebuilt, so what's left are craft gaps between screens: exits, endings and continuity. That's Jakub's territory, so his lens set the order. Jhey marked the one moment to invest in, and Emil only weighed in on the daily home screen.

  • Lean EmilCut the drop-away and the home reveal to under 250ms, and question the ripple and tick for anything beyond the video.
  • Lean JakubPair every enter with a quieter exit, give every conditional element an ending, and keep all motion on transforms and opacity.
  • Lean JheyOver-invest in the payoff: carry the counter into the header, and let each benefit found light up the card it came from.
